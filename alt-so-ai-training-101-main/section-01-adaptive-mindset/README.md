# Section 01: Adaptive Mindset

**No lab. No setup. Just a reframe.**

---

## Why This Comes First

Before you touch a tool or open a terminal, this section asks you to do something harder: examine how you think about your work.

AI doesn't change the fundamentals of good product leadership — understanding users, making decisions with incomplete information, building alignment across teams. What it changes is the *speed* and *scale* at which you can operate. That shift rewards certain habits and exposes others.

This section is a primer on the mindset that makes the difference.

---

## The Shift: From Producer to Director

Most of us are trained to think of our value as what we *produce* — the document, the research, the plan, the slide. AI changes that equation. The output is cheap. The judgment about what to produce, when, for whom, and how good is "good enough" — that's where your value lives now.

The mental model that works:

> **You are the director. AI is a fast, capable, and occasionally overconfident collaborator.**

A good director doesn't do everything themselves. They give clear briefs, review output critically, redirect when something's off, and make the calls their collaborators can't. The labs in this training are practice at exactly that.

---

## Three Habits That Matter

**1. Front-load your judgment**
The biggest leverage point is *before* you prompt. What is this really for? Who will read it? What does "good" look like? A clear brief produces better output than five rounds of correction. The Signal Assessment in Section 03 is a direct application of this.

**2. Review like an editor, not an author**
When AI produces a draft, your job shifts to critique. Read it skeptically. Ask: Is this accurate? Is this specific enough? Does it reflect what the stakeholder actually said, or a plausible-sounding version of it? The habit of treating AI output as a first draft — not a final one — is the single most important quality gate you have.

**3. Iterate in public**
Version control is not a developer habit. It's a thinking habit. Committing your work at each step creates a record of your reasoning, makes collaboration possible, and gives you a safety net to experiment without fear of losing progress. Every lab in this training ends with a commit.

---

## What This Training Is Built On

The labs in Sections 02–05 follow a single through-line:

> **Raw materials → Structured context → Deliverable → Reusable workflow**

Each lab is a practice rep on one part of that chain. By the end, you'll have done the full cycle end-to-end using real content from a real nonprofit. The skills transfer directly to your client work.

---

## Before You Continue

No action items. No worksheet.

Just carry this question into each lab:

> *Where is my judgment adding value here — and where am I just reviewing output?*

When you can answer that clearly for each step you take, you're working the way AI-enabled product leaders work.
