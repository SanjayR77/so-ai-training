# Mary's Place Operational Challenges

## Shelter Services
- Length of stay is increasing amid housing affordability and economic pressures in King County.
- Intake is manual and requires separate assessments by health, housing, and youth-services staff.
- There is no centralized map of a family's journey to estimate timelines or sequence complex steps toward housing.
- The internal database is outdated, clunky, and requires significant customization.
- Staff enter information twice, in the internal database and county-wide Homelessness Management Information System (HMIS); the systems are not integrated.

## Mobile Outreach Services
- Outreach teams cannot reliably provide household goods to families.
- Specialists request items by calling the warehouse manager, without visibility into available inventory.
- Fulfillment depends on what happens to be available rather than each family's needs.
- Families may give only a few days' notice before moving into housing, limiting time to source needed items.

## Prevention Services
- There is no established process for distributing goods to prevention families.
- Prevention families cannot access the item marketplace available to shelter guests.

## Goods Management and Distribution (Cross-Cutting)
- Only 15-20% of donated items are usable by Mary's Place families.
- Inventory is tracked manually by bin counts, without SKU-level detail.
- Without real-time needs data, the organization cannot effectively target corporate donations.
- Unusable surplus accumulates because there is no efficient process for redistributing it to other community organizations.
- Outreach specialist offices have limited storage capacity.
- Supply information from the warehouse is not efficiently connected to family demand, and demand is not tracked centrally across shelter, outreach, and prevention.

## Open Questions for Workflow Discovery

### Shelter Services
- Which intake steps, handoffs, and assessment tasks create the most delay or duplicate work? What are the baseline durations and exceptions?
- How are shelter eligibility, bed availability, and family prioritization determined, and which system is the source of truth?

### Mobile Outreach and Prevention
- Who identifies, requests, approves, assembles, delivers, and receives goods for non-shelter families? What alternatives exist when items are unavailable?
- What eligibility, access, and fulfillment process would work for prevention families, who currently lack an established goods-distribution path?
- What notice, transportation, storage, and accessibility constraints affect families receiving goods?

### Goods Management and Distribution
- What criteria define a usable donation, and how are items sorted, valued, tracked, and recorded as distributed or disposed?
- What are the volumes and turnaround times for donations, family requests, fulfillment, and surplus redistribution?
- Which community partners can receive surplus, and what logistics, costs, or safeguards govern redistribution?
- Which outcomes should be measured, such as inventory accuracy, usable-donation rate, fulfillment time, or unmet demand?
