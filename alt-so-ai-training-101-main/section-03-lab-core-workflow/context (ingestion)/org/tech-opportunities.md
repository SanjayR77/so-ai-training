# Technology Opportunities and System Priorities

## Family Intake and Housing Journey
- **Proposed concept: “shelter virtual advocate.”** A family-facing, AI-supported experience could collect intake information at the family's pace, then organize complex needs into prioritized, manageable steps.
- Draw on health, housing, and youth-services assessments to recommend what to address first and when; some barriers take months, so sequencing may help families and staff use time effectively.
- Provide a flexible estimate of the family's path and timeline toward housing. Stakeholders emphasized tailoring the plan to each family's needs, not imposing a fixed shelter stay limit.
- Support communication across the 4-8 languages and cultural backgrounds represented at a given time; stakeholders estimated 12-15 languages could cover the broader range. Communication style should be familiar to families.
- Enable a family’s housing specialist and relevant service teams to see shared goals and progress, so staff can focus on the important work together. The current shared data system is clunky.
- Potential benefits raised: make the journey easier to understand, build families’ skills for tackling complex problems, and improve shelter throughput and internal capacity-and-demand forecasting.

## Goods Inventory, Requests, and Distribution
- **Proposed system priority: coordinate supply and demand across shelter, outreach, and prevention.** Stakeholders described the need as centralized, streamlined, systematized information flow, rather than simply automating existing processes.
- Establish usable inventory information; there is currently no information system, SKU-level tracking, barcode scanning, or item-level counts. Warehouse counts are done by category/bin for audit valuation, with donated food measured by weight.
- Give families or staff a way to view available goods or submit requests early enough to source items. Requests for non-shelter families are currently ad hoc calls to the warehouse, often with only a few days' notice; available lead time can vary.
- Improve matching and fulfillment based on actual family needs, rather than what happens to be on hand when a request arrives. Better information could also help staff fulfill needs after move-in when items are not immediately available.
- Consider a physical **marketplace** at a facility for outreach and prevention families, modeled on the shelter marketplaces where guests can shop. Stakeholders raised this alongside online inventory/request access, potentially as complementary options.
- Coordinate with **Make a Home**, the existing program in which a staff member sources household items for families moving into housing, including shelter, outreach, and prevention families.
- Track needs across service areas to focus corporate giving on current demand, distinguish useful donations from surplus, and route usable surplus to other community organizations. The current generic Amazon wish lists do not provide this level of targeting.
- Account for limited storage at outreach offices and the logistics of moving items from donor to warehouse to family or community partner.

## Current Systems and Data Constraints
- Mary's Place uses a shared internal client data system across teams; staff can see family goals and work by other teams. The current application is an old, open-source SQL database model, customized in-house, and described as clunky and dated.
- Staff manually enter basic client demographics into the county-wide Homelessness Management Information System (HMIS) as well as the internal system. There is no integration; HMIS receives minimal demographic information, not individual goals.
- Stakeholders said no external parties have access to client goals in the internal system.
- Internal IT primarily manages hardware and software contracts. A data team stewards collection, analysis, evaluation, the existing system, queries, and reports.
- Inventory processes are shaped partly by financial audit requirements to count and value received, consumed, distributed, and disposed in-kind goods.

## Technology Relationships and Constraints
- Mary's Place is Microsoft-grounded, using Windows and Microsoft Office, and is training and experimenting with Microsoft Copilot. Current Copilot work includes basic HR chatbot use cases for benefits, the employee handbook, and policies.
- Stakeholders said they do not have a sophisticated Azure cloud-based technology stack. Whether infrastructure is deployed in AWS was unknown to participants.
- Mary's Place has a close relationship with Amazon. Amazon Legal works with law firms providing pro bono support; Amazon also built a family shelter on its campus.
- A prior pro bono effort involving Amazon contacts and a law firm produced a multilingual, trauma-informed chatbot that walked asylum-seeking families through complex applications. It reduced attorney meetings from about four hours to one hour. The law firm owns the tool; Mary's Place does not manage it, and stakeholders said the asylum paperwork need that prompted it had since passed.

## Named Concepts and Desired Outcome
- **“Shelter virtual advocate”** is Jason's tentative name for the family intake and journey-support idea.
- **Make a Home** is an existing household-goods sourcing program that a goods system could coordinate with.
- Shelter **marketplaces** are an existing distribution model; a facility-based version for outreach and prevention was proposed.
- Stakeholders said a prototype or sufficiently developed concept to discuss and shop around for funding would be useful. They welcomed follow-up discovery with care-coordination and goods-intake/distribution staff.
